    Four levels

    Organisation, donor, grant, project

    • ALC signs in once and sees every donor
    • Each grant is its own fund with its own rules
    • Each project knows which grant pays for it
    • A shared cost is split between grants, never charged twice

    What you gain: "how much of this donor's money is left?" is answered from the books, not from a spreadsheet.

    Step 2 of 6

    Organisation

    ALC in Tranzlo

    • Your own database, separate from every other customer
    • Head office and field offices as locations
    • Staff see only the projects they work on
    • One chart of accounts for every grant

    What you gain: a field officer in Mithi never sees Swat's costs, and management sees everything.

    Step 3 of 6

    • The donor's rules: report format, dates, allowed costs

    Step 1 of 6

    Five controls

    The same on every document, in every module

    • Only the right people can create or change it
    • It waits for approval before it takes effect
    • It is linked to what came before and after
    • Its papers stay attached to it
    • Every change is recorded

    What you gain: the donor's auditor finds the answer on the document itself.

    Step 2 of 6

    Permissions

    Who can open a screen, and which projects they see

    • View, create, edit and delete given separately, screen by screen
    • Ready-made roles for finance, programmes and the field
    • Project-level access: staff see only their own projects
    • Access also limited by office

    What you gain: each person sees only the grants and projects their job needs.

    Step 3 of 6

    Approvals

    You decide which documents wait, and for whom

    • Set per document type, department and office
    • Several levels, each with its own approvers
    • Rules by amount
    • Proposals, amendments and donor reports go through approval too
    • One My Approvals inbox, on the phone as well

    What you gain: segregation of duties that a donor audit expects, without paper files moving between desks.

    Step 4 of 6

    Linked documents

    Each document is made from the one before it

    • Requisition, order, goods received, invoice, payment
    • The whole chain shows on any document in it
    • Open a linked document in one click
    • Print the full chain for the audit file

    What you gain: the procurement trail a donor asks for is one print, not a folder search.

    Step 5 of 6

    Attachments

    The papers live with the document

    • Quotations, contracts and receipts on the document
    • Files can be added after the document is locked
    • Receipt photos taken on the phone in the field
    • Remarks kept as a running log

    What you gain: the evidence behind every cost is where the cost is.

    Step 6 of 6

    Audit log

    Written by the system, not by the user

    • Every save recorded: who, when, old and new value
    • Approve, post and lock actions recorded too
    • Moving a cost from one grant to another shows up here
    • One Logs screen to search it all

    What you gain: nobody can quietly move a cost to the donor with money left.

    Step 1 of 6

    Five common leaks

    Seen in most organisations that run several grants

    • Money from different donors is mixed
    • Shared costs are split by guess
    • Field cash has no trail
    • Donor reports are rebuilt by hand
    • Deadlines and donor rules depend on memory

    The cost: disallowed costs paid back to the donor, late reports, and trust lost at renewal time.

    Step 2 of 6

    Money mixed

    "Which donor paid for this?"

    • By hand: answered by searching files, often wrongly
    • Tranzlo: every voucher, purchase and salary line carries its grant
    • Received, spent and remaining per grant, any day

    What you gain: each donor's fund balance is always known and always matches the books.

    Step 3 of 6

    Shared costs

    One coordinator works on three grants

    • By hand: split decided when the report is due
    • Tranzlo: the split is set on the employee, dated, and applied by payroll
    • A bill can be split between grants on the voucher itself

    What you gain: no cost is charged twice, and every split can be explained.

    Step 4 of 6

    Field cash

    Money handed to staff for field work

    • By hand: given in cash, settled weeks later, receipts missing
    • Tranzlo: each float numbered, spends recorded on the phone with a photo
    • Accounts approves line by line and the rest comes back

    What you gain: every amount in the field has a receipt and a grant. We show this live.

    Step 5 of 6

    Donor reports

    Each donor wants its own format and dates

    • By hand: copy figures from accounts, results from field sheets
    • Tranzlo: figures from the ledger, results from the results entry
    • Once submitted, the report never changes

    What you gain: what the donor received can always be shown again, exactly.

    Step 6 of 6

    Deadlines and rules

    The donor agreement as working rules

    • Report schedule made from the agreement
    • Reminders before reports, milestones and grant end
    • Spending outside the grant dates or on a not-allowed account is caught when entered

    What you gain: nothing depends on one person remembering a date.

    Step 1 of 7

    Fund accounting

    Each grant is its own fund inside one set of books

    • Every cost says which grant and project paid for it
    • Shared bills and salaries split by share
    • The donor's rules checked when a cost is entered
    • Budget and balance per grant, any day

    What you gain: one accounts team runs every donor's money without a spreadsheet per donor.

    Step 2 of 7

    Tagged at the source

    The grant is chosen on the document, not later

    • Purchase orders, invoices, vouchers and floats carry grant and project
    • The tag flows into the ledger by itself
    • A line with no grant is core funds
    • Moving a cost between grants is recorded in the audit log

    What you gain: "which donor paid for this?" has one answer, already in the books.

    Step 3 of 7

    Split bill

    One vehicle serves three grants

    • Split the line by percentage or amount
    • Each share is posted to its own grant and project
    • The split reopens together if the voucher is edited

    What you gain: each donor pays its fair share, and the split is on record.

    Step 4 of 7

    Salary split

    Staff who work on several grants

    • Split set on the employee, with a start date
    • Payroll applies it to the salary journal every month
    • Deductions follow the same split
    • Changing the split later does not change past months

    What you gain: salary charged to donors is consistent month after month, and auditable.

    Step 5 of 7

    Donor rules

    The agreement, checked on every entry

    • Cost dated outside the grant period
    • Account the donor does not allow
    • Grant not yet active, or already closed
    • You choose: warn the user, or stop the entry

    What you gain: disallowed costs are caught at entry, not found by the donor's auditor.

    Step 6 of 7

    Grant budget

    Over the grant's own period

    • Budget by line for the whole grant, not your fiscal year
    • Spent and on order against each line
    • Burn rate and forecast to the grant's end
    • Alert when a line passes a set percentage

    What you gain: over- and under-spending show up months before the final report.

    Step 7 of 7

    Fund balance

    What each donor gave, and what is left

    • Received per tranche, from the bank receipts
    • Spent and on order from the ledger
    • Cash still held for the donor
    • Income recognised as the grant is spent

    What you gain: you never spend one donor's money on another donor's project by accident.

    Money for field work

    Not a salary advance: money to spend for ALC

    • Settle and return: for a trip or a month; the rest comes back and the float closes
    • Imprest: a fixed petty cash at a field office, topped back up by what was approved
    • One person can hold more than one float
    • Every spend carries its grant and project

    What you gain: every amount in the field has a person, a receipt and a grant.

    Step 2 of 9

    Request

    Bilal asks from his phone, or Accounts creates it

    • Purpose, dates and amount
    • Grant and project the spends will go to
    • Limit on what one person may hold, if you set one
    • A new float can be refused while an old one is overdue

    What you gain: no cash leaves the office without a numbered request.

    Step 3 of 9

    Approve

    Your rule decides

    • Approval workflow set for the department or office: it waits in My Approvals
    • No workflow set: approved at once
    • Approvers can be by amount and level

    What you gain: large floats need sign-off; small ones do not slow the field down.

    Step 4 of 9

    Pay out

    Accounts pays from cash or bank

    • The cash payment is made by the float, not typed separately
    • It goes to Expense floats, against Bilal
    • Not an expense yet: the money is still ALC's, in Bilal's hands

    What you gain: how much cash each person holds is known every day.

    Step 5 of 9

    Spend

    Recorded on the phone, the same day

    • Date, expense head, vendor, amount
    • Photo of the receipt from the camera
    • Grant and project on each line
    • Works on any phone browser; no app to install

    What you gain: receipts are not lost in a pocket for three weeks.

    Step 6 of 9

    Submit

    All recorded spends go together

    • A receipt is required above the amount you set
    • Flags for Accounts only: same receipt twice, spend outside the float dates, same vendor, date and amount on another float, no receipt
    • Bilal can recall it until Accounts posts

    What you gain: suspicious spends are pointed out to the checker, without accusing anyone.

    Step 7 of 9

    Review

    Accounts approves line by line

    • Each spend approved or rejected with a reason
    • The miscellaneous 1,000 has no receipt: rejected
    • A rejected spend is not an expense; it stays on Bilal's balance
    • Bilal can never approve or post his own spends

    What you gain: the person who spends is never the person who approves.

    Step 8 of 9

    Post

    One journal for the whole settlement

    • Each spend to its own expense head
    • Each line on grant NWF-2026-01 and the Clean water project
    • Expense recorded only now, after approval

    What you gain: the donor's grant shows the field spend the day it is approved.

    Step 9 of 9

    Close

    One click: "Receive 5,300 from Bilal"

    • Cash receipt posted; balance 0; the float closes by itself
    • Spent more than given? Accounts pays it back the same way
    • Imprest: Accounts tops it back up by the approved amount
    • A mistake? Undo the latest entry and the balance corrects itself

    What you gain: no float stays open and forgotten.

    Presenter: the live demo after this page
    1. Before the demo: Finance → Account links, check "Expense floats with employees" is mapped; set the receipt amount (e.g. 2,000); add a demo grant and project.
    2. Sign in as the field officer on a phone: Self-service → My Floats → request 30,000 for the trip, grant and project chosen.
    3. If a float workflow is set, approve it from My Approvals; then as Accounts: Finance → Expense Floats → pay out in cash.
    4. Back on the phone: add three spends with a receipt photo, and one of 1,000 without a receipt. Submit as final.
    5. As Accounts: open the float; show the "No receipt" flag; reject that line with a reason, approve the rest; Post.
    6. Click "Receive 5,300 from Bilal": the float closes.
    7. Open the journal: grant and project on each line. Then the Expense Float Report: outstanding, and the ledger check.
    8. Optional: Undo the receipt to show the way back, then receive it again.
